## 1. Governing Rule

Stage 2 thinks, understands, analyzes, challenges, and simulates — it does not decide or execute.

- Evidence → Stage 11
- Research → Stage 12
- Final Decision → Stage 13
- Execution → Stage 14
- Signals & Market Intelligence → Stage 15
- Bot Construction / Coordination / Consumption → Stage 16

Capability ≠ Engine ≠ Gate.

Capability = an independent cognitive ability.
Engine = an operational component that runs multiple capabilities.
Gate = a constitutional barrier that prevents invalid output from crossing a boundary.

## 2. Phase 1 — Trading (28 Capabilities)

### 1. Intent Understanding
Identifies what the user actually wants and classifies the request.
Input: user message + context.
Process: intent extraction and request classification.
Output: Intent Model.
Boundary: no final decision or execution authority.

### 2. Semantic & Literal Understanding
Understands words, sentences, literal meaning, intended meaning, ambiguity, and figurative language.
Input: text / symbols.
Process: semantic parsing + disambiguation.
Output: Meaning Model.
Boundary: no evidence validation or external research authority.

### 3. Context Awareness
Understands the current message as part of the active conversation and task context.
Input: current message + relevant history.
Process: context linking, active-topic detection, stage/task identification, asset/timeframe resolution.
Output: Active Context.
Boundary: no market-intelligence authority.

### 4. Temporal Awareness
Maintains temporal ordering and separates past, present, and future.
Input: events, timestamps, temporal expressions.
Process: ordering, age calculation, temporal classification.
Output: Temporal Model.
Boundary: future states are not facts.

### 5. Goal Awareness
Identifies final, current, and sub-goals and detects priority conflicts or drift.
Input: intent + context.
Process: goal decomposition and alignment.
Output: Goal Model.
Boundary: goal is not a Stage 13 decision.

### 6. Knowledge-State Awareness
Distinguishes known information from inference, hypothesis, and assumption and tracks provenance/age.
Input: cognitive state.
Process: epistemic classification.
Output: Knowledge State.
Boundary: inference cannot become evidence by assertion.

### 7. Knowledge-Gap Detection
Detects missing information and classifies whether the gap is critical.
Input: required information + available information.
Process: gap analysis.
Output: Knowledge Gaps / missing-data request.
Boundary: does not perform Stage 12 research.

### 8. Uncertainty Awareness
Represents uncertainty, its source, and how it can be reduced.
Input: information + interpretations.
Process: uncertainty classification and update.
Output: Uncertainty State.
Boundary: not a replacement for Stage 11 evidence/confidence authority.

### 9. Evidence-Need Awareness
Determines what claims require proof and what kind of evidence or research is needed.
Input: claim / hypothesis / reasoning state.
Process: evidence-requirement generation.
Output: Evidence Requirement / Research Request.
Boundary: identifies need; Stage 11/12 performs its authorized work.

### 10. Structural Analysis
Decomposes problems into components, relationships, dependencies, and priorities.
Input: problem.
Process: decomposition and structural mapping.
Output: Structural Model.
Boundary: no final decision.

### 11. Pattern Analysis
Detects patterns, repetitions, exceptions, and similarities.
Input: available structured information.
Process: pattern extraction and comparison.
Output: Pattern Model.
Boundary: does not become Stage 15 signal authority.

### 12. Causal Candidate Analysis
Generates and compares possible causes and chains of influence while separating correlation from causation.
Input: events + relationships.
Process: causal candidate analysis.
Output: Causal Candidates.
Boundary: inferred causality is not verified fact.

### 13. Consequence Analysis
Analyzes direct, indirect, side, reversible, permanent, and affected-party consequences.
Input: state + possible change/action.
Process: consequence mapping.
Output: Consequence Model.
Boundary: does not execute actions.

### 14. Information Sufficiency Analysis
Determines whether available information is sufficient, insufficient, or unknown for the intended reasoning task.
Input: required vs available information.
Process: sufficiency analysis.
Output: Sufficient / Insufficient / Unknown + gaps.
Boundary: sufficiency does not grant decision authority.

### 15. Hypothesis & Counter-Hypothesis Reasoning
Builds a hypothesis, counter-hypothesis, and alternatives.
Input: problem + observations.
Process: competing-hypothesis generation and comparison.
Output: Competing Hypotheses.
Boundary: hypothesis is not evidence or final decision.

### 16. Hypothesis Falsification
Attempts to disprove a hypothesis and records what weakens or leaves it unresolved.
Input: hypothesis.
Process: falsification tests.
Output: Supported / Weakened / Unresolved.
Boundary: does not replace Stage 11 verification.

### 17. Counterfactual Reasoning
Tests “what if?” changes to assumptions or variables.
Input: current model + changed assumption.
Process: recomputation and sensitivity comparison.
Output: Counterfactual Model.
Boundary: counterfactual is not reality.

### 18. Assumption Detection
Extracts explicit and implicit assumptions and tests their weakness.
Input: reasoning chain.
Process: assumption extraction, classification, challenge.
Output: Assumption Set.
Boundary: assumptions cannot be presented as facts.

### 19. Proof-Bound Cognition
Every cognitive result carries an epistemic status.
Input: any cognitive output.
Process: classify as Observed, Fact from Authorized Source, Interpretation, Inference, Hypothesis, Assumption, Simulation, Forecast, Unknown, or Unverified.
Output: Proof-Bound Cognitive State.
Boundary: imagination/simulation/forecast cannot become fact or evidence.

### 20. Contradiction Detection & Handling
Detects, classifies, localizes, compares, challenges, tests, and records contradictions.
Input: conflicting information/reasoning.
Process: detect → classify → localize → compare → challenge → counter-argument → evidence need → test → evaluate → accept/reject/defer → record.
Output: Contradiction Record + disposition.
Boundary: external evidence conflicts remain under Stage 11 authority.

### 21. Reasoning Chain Validation
Validates links between premises, assumptions, intermediate steps, and conclusions.
Input: reasoning chain.
Process: detect missing premises, unsupported jumps, invalid transitions.
Output: Valid / Invalid / Incomplete Chain.
Boundary: internal reasoning validation, not evidence verification.

### 22. Cross-Reference Resolution
Resolves references such as “this”, “the previous one”, “same strategy”, or “that position”.
Input: reference + conversation context.
Process: entity/reference resolution.
Output: Resolved Reference.
Boundary: cannot invent an antecedent.

### 23. Market Context Understanding
Interprets market context supplied by authorized inputs.
Input: market context.
Process: asset, timeframe, market-state, and terminology interpretation.
Output: Market Context Model.
Boundary: does not process live market intelligence as Stage 15.

### 24. Risk Language Detection
Detects risk-related language and user risk intent.
Input: user language + trading context.
Process: identify risk, loss, stop, exposure, leverage, etc.
Output: Risk Intent Signals.
Boundary: no authorization or risk execution; Stage 14 owns that.

### 25. Strategy Reference Resolution
Resolves abbreviated or historical references to a strategy/system.
Input: strategy reference + context.
Process: reference matching.
Output: Strategy Reference Model.
Boundary: does not determine strategy validity.

### 26. Position State Awareness
Understands position state when authorized position information is available.
Input: position information.
Process: state interpretation.
Output: Position Context Model.
Boundary: does not modify or become the execution source of truth.

### 27. Source Credibility Assessment
Assesses source characteristics and relevance without becoming the verification authority.
Input: source metadata/context.
Process: credibility/relevance assessment.
Output: Source Credibility Assessment.
Boundary: assessment ≠ verification; Stage 11 owns verification.

### 28. Input Bias Detection
Detects framing, anchoring, selective assumptions, and other bias signals in incoming information.
Input: user/input content.
Process: bias analysis.
Output: Input Bias Signals.
Boundary: flags influence on reasoning; does not judge the user.

## 3. Phase 2 — Awareness & Perception (30 Capabilities)

### 29. User-State Modeling
Builds a probabilistic model of the user's current state.
Input: language + context + history.
Process: state inference and update.
Output: User-State Model.
Boundary: inference, not mind reading.

### 30. Emotional Signal Interpretation
Extracts emotional indicators from language, punctuation, emoji, and context.
Input: communication signals.
Process: signal interpretation.
Output: Emotional Signal Candidates.
Boundary: not a claim about actual internal emotion.

### 31. Tone Shift Detection
Detects changes in communication tone/style.
Input: current + previous communication.
Process: comparison.
Output: Tone Shift Signal.

### 32. Emoji & Symbol Context Interpretation
Interprets emoji and symbols using surrounding language and context.
Input: emoji/symbol + text + context.
Process: contextual interpretation.
Output: Emoji Meaning Candidate.

### 33. Implicit Meaning Detection
Generates supported interpretations of what is implied but not explicitly stated.
Input: explicit content + context.
Process: pragmatic inference + alternatives.
Output: Implicit Meaning Candidates.
Boundary: implicit inference remains inference/hypothesis.

### 34. Hint Interpretation
Turns short hints into candidate meanings using context.
Input: hint + context.
Process: signal interpretation.
Output: Intent/Meaning Candidate.

### 35. Silence Interpretation
Treats silence or absence of response as a possible contextual signal only when supported.
Input: interaction timing + history.
Process: silence analysis.
Output: Possible Interpretations / Unknown.
Boundary: silence does not automatically mean agreement, anger, or refusal.

### 36. Attention & Relevance Awareness
Identifies what is currently important and prioritizes relevant signals.
Input: goals + context + signals.
Process: relevance prioritization.
Output: Attention Model.

### 37. Relationship Awareness
Models relationships between entities, events, goals, and states.
Input: entities + events + goals.
Process: relationship mapping.
Output: Relationship Model.

### 38. Continuity Awareness
Maintains continuity of understanding across interaction boundaries.
Input: previous context + current input.
Process: retrieve + compare + integrate.
Output: Continuity Model.

### 39. Cognitive State Persistence
Updates and persists the cognitive state as new information arrives.
Input: old state + new information.
Process: compare → revise → version → persist.
Output: Updated Cognitive State.
Boundary: persistence does not grant authority.

### 40. Cognitive Position Revision
Revises a prior cognitive position when new information provides sufficient reason.
Input: existing position + new information.
Process: reassessment.
Output: Revised Position + Reason.
Boundary: revision must remain proof-bound.

### 41. Theory-of-Mind Modeling
Builds a probabilistic model of what the user may know, want, expect, or assume.
Input: interaction signals + context.
Process: candidate mental-state modeling.
Output: Mind Model.
Boundary: model, not mind reading.

### 42. Communication-Style Awareness
Learns interaction style and adapts communication accordingly.
Input: communication history.
Process: style analysis.
Output: Communication Style Model.

### 43. User-Context Modeling
Fuses user-state signals with the active context.
Input: User State + Context.
Process: contextual fusion.
Output: User-Context Model.

### 44. Empathy Modeling
Simulates communication approaches likely to be more suitable to the modeled user state.
Input: User-State Model.
Process: response-style simulation.
Output: Communication Adaptation Candidate.
Boundary: simulated empathy is not claimed subjective emotion.

### 45. Multi-Perspective Analysis
Analyzes a problem from multiple relevant perspectives.
Input: problem + context.
Process: generate perspectives → compare → identify conflicts.
Output: Perspective Set + Differences.
Boundary: no independent decision authority.

### 46. Knowledge Synthesis
Integrates multiple knowledge elements while preserving provenance and disagreements.
Input: multiple knowledge elements.
Process: integrate + reconcile where possible.
Output: Synthesized Knowledge Model.
Boundary: synthesis ≠ verification.

### 47. Mental Model Construction
Builds and updates an internal model of how a system/problem/environment works.
Input: concepts + relationships + observations.
Process: model construction/update.
Output: Mental Model.
Boundary: model ≠ reality.

### 48. Sarcasm Detection
Detects probable sarcasm when literal meaning conflicts with context/tone.
Input: text + context + tone.
Process: literal/pragmatic comparison.
Output: Sarcasm Candidate + uncertainty.
Boundary: no certainty without sufficient cues.

### 49. Irony Detection
Detects probable irony or contextual meaning that conflicts with literal wording.
Input: text + context.
Process: pragmatic contradiction analysis.
Output: Irony Candidate.

### 50. Humor Recognition
Recognizes probable humor/joking content.
Input: text + context.
Process: pragmatic interpretation.
Output: Humor Signal.

### 51. Cultural Context Interpretation
Uses relevant cultural context to interpret expressions without assuming every individual follows a cultural norm.
Input: language + cultural context.
Process: contextual semantic/pragmatic mapping.
Output: Cultural Interpretation Candidates.

### 52. Multi-Language Switching
Maintains semantic continuity when the user switches languages.
Input: multilingual conversation.
Process: language-state tracking + semantic integration.
Output: Unified Meaning Model.

### 53. Code-Switching Detection
Detects switching between languages, dialects, and technical vocabularies within an interaction.
Input: mixed-language input.
Process: segmentation + classification + integration.
Output: Code-Switch Map.

### 54. Pause Detection
Treats available interaction pauses as temporal signals.
Input: interaction timing.
Process: pause-pattern analysis.
Output: Pause Signal.
Boundary: does not infer the reason for a pause.

### 55. Hesitation Detection
Detects linguistic indicators of hesitation.
Input: wording + revisions + available timing.
Process: hesitation-pattern analysis.
Output: Hesitation Signal.
Boundary: signal, not confirmed mental state.

### 56. Confirmation Signal Detection
Detects probable confirmation in user responses.
Input: response + context.
Process: confirmation classification.
Output: Confirmation Candidate.
Boundary: confirmation is not execution authorization.

### 57. Correction Detection
Detects when the user corrects a prior interpretation or fact.
Input: new statement + previous state.
Process: compare → detect correction → update.
Output: Correction Event.

### 58. Meta-Communication Understanding
Understands communication about communication itself.
Input: message + interaction behavior.
Process: distinguish content from communication instructions.
Output: Meta-Communication State.
Boundary: communication instructions do not become execution authority.

## 6. Gates (3)

### Gate 1 — Knowledge Integrity / Proof-Bound Gate
Every cognitive output must carry its epistemic classification. It blocks unsupported transitions between observation, fact, interpretation, inference, hypothesis, assumption, simulation, forecast, unknown, and unverified.

### Gate 2 — Cognitive Integrity Gate
Blocks unsupported claims, hidden contradictions, unjustified logical jumps, and conversion of guesses into knowledge.

### Gate 3 — Authority & Simulation Boundary Gate
Blocks Stage 2 output from being represented as Evidence, Final Decision, Authorization, Execution, or Stage 15 Market Signal Authority.

## 7. Boundary with Stages 11–16

Stage 11 = Evidence + Verification authority.
Stage 12 = External Research authority.
Stage 13 = Final Decision + Decision Reasoning authority.
Stage 14 = Authorization + Execution + Broker authority.
Stage 15 = Market Intelligence + Signal Processing authority.
Stage 16 = Bot Construction + Coordination + Consumption authority.

Stage 2 may understand market context, user trading intent, risk language, strategy references, and position state when supplied by authorized inputs. It may analyze, hypothesize, challenge, simulate, and identify evidence needs. It may not assume the authority of any of Stages 11–16.

Core boundary:
“Stage 2 can infer what was not said, but cannot claim that an inference was said.”
“It can build a model of the user's mind, but cannot claim to have read the user's mind.”
“It can challenge and revise its reasoning, but persistence does not make a belief true.”
“It can simulate and forecast, but cannot convert simulation or forecast into reality or evidence.”

## 8. Cognitive State

The Cognitive State concept is a state model, not a Capability, Engine, or Gate.

Core fields:
Identity Context; Role; Current State; Context; Current Intent; Goal; Known Information; Unknown Information; Uncertainty; Assumptions; Hypotheses; Counter-Hypotheses; Contradictions; Reasoning State; User-State Model; Mind Model; Emotional Signals; Tone Signals; Attention Signals; Conversation Continuity; Cognitive Changes; Provenance; Timestamp; Version.

Important values should carry:
Value; Source; Source Type; Observed/Inferred; Timestamp; Uncertainty; Expiration; Revision History.

## 9. Mind Model Constitutional Rule

Stage 2 builds a probabilistic model of the user's state, intent, goals, preferences, communication style, emotional/tone/attention signals, unresolved requests, patterns, contradictions, and uncertainty.

It does not claim direct access to the user's mind.

Every inferred user-state value must remain identifiable as observed, inferred, hypothesized, or unknown according to its provenance.
